\begin{abstract}
Given a fixed straight-line drawing of a complete graph, we seek a
two-colouring of its edges that minimizes the number of monochromatic
crossing pairs. We prove that the threshold decision problem is
NP-complete, even when the vertices are in general position and have
integer coordinates of polynomial binary length. Thus exact minimization
is NP-hard, answering the complexity question posed at the Crossing
Number Workshop in 2019. The reduction is from MaxCut on permutation
graphs. Its main difficulty is to control the edges required by
completeness, whose colours must remain free. We construct a large
comparison grid with two stable colour phases and use additional points
to assign costs to the edges that encode the source graph. A geometric
separation eliminates one large class of unwanted crossings. The costs
of all remaining edges are included in a computable baseline and bounded
errors, with each unit of the source objective amplified beyond the total
error. The construction is deterministic and has polynomial size and
coordinate bit length.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Complete geometric graph; monochromatic crossings; permutation graph; MaxCut; quantitative stability.
